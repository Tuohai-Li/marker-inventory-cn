# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Personal marker and drawing-tool collectors who want a pleasant way to catalog what they own, track stock, remember purchases, manage brands and series, save wishlist items, and review their collection through charts and color swatches. They are likely using the app in a hobby or studio-adjacent context where the collection itself is tactile, visual, and personal.

## Product Purpose

This product is a hand-drawn marker inventory notebook for the browser. It helps users turn scattered marker information into an organized collection: searchable library, inventory status, brand and series records, purchase history, wishlist, statistics, export, and settings.

Success means the app feels less like generic inventory software and more like opening a real notebook on a desk: the user can flip through pages, see their collection drawn into the paper surface, and still complete practical management tasks quickly and clearly. The current notebook metaphor is core to the product, and future work may explore a more dimensional or 3D book body.

## Brand Personality

Handmade, clear, quiet. The interface should feel tactile and personal without becoming messy; expressive enough to honor art supplies, disciplined enough to support repeated inventory work.

## Anti-references

Do not make it look like a generic admin dashboard, enterprise inventory system, ecommerce landing page, or overly polished SaaS console. Avoid designs where the notebook is only decoration around a conventional app. 

## Design Principles

- The notebook is the product frame, not a background theme.
- Hand-drawn details must clarify the task or strengthen the collection metaphor.
- Practical workflows stay readable, searchable, and fast.
- Page turns and book structure should orient the user, not slow them down.
- Preserve tactile charm while making forms, tables, charts, and navigation trustworthy.

## 3D Scene Vision

The interface should evolve into a complete three-dimensional desk scene rather than placing a decorative 3D background behind a conventional web app. Existing functional pages become the readable, interactive paper pages of an open physical book. The book is the primary product frame and should have a clearly modeled cover, spine, binding, page thickness, rounded edges, and a soft shadow that anchors it to the desk.

The open book sits on an artist's desk in front of a window. Outside the window, restrained flowers, foliage, and trees create a fresh natural view without competing with the page content. Marker pens, a pen cup, color swatches, a sketchbook, and a few drawing tools sit near the book to reinforce the personal studio setting and the marker-collection subject.

The visual direction is fresh, quiet, tactile, and lightly handmade: soft daylight, warm paper white, low-saturation greens, gentle wood tones, and controlled hand-drawn details. Use a stylized semi-realistic 3D treatment with the refinement of a miniature artist's workspace, avoiding both toy-like cartoon styling and highly photorealistic interior rendering.

The preferred camera is a slightly elevated three-quarter view. It must keep the book pages readable while revealing the book's depth, desk surface, window frame, surrounding tools, and the layered outdoor scenery. Composition, materials, and lighting should support practical interaction first; scenery remains calm and secondary to the open pages.

Concept imagery for this direction should function as a Three.js production reference, clearly communicating spatial hierarchy, object proportions, material zones, lighting direction, and depth layers rather than serving only as an atmospheric illustration.
